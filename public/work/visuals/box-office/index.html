Box office, by the numbers.

    From the data

    More money, fewer kinds of movies.

    My takeaway: we pay far more for a narrower slate of movies that earn less per dollar. Every number here is calculated from the films on this page, in 2025 dollars. Nothing is estimated.

    01

    Costs are way up

    After inflation, the median budget went from $64M (1991–1995) to $106M (2021–2025), up 64%. Films costing $100M or more went from 23% of the list to 57%.

    02

    Less back per dollar

    The typical film grossed 4.1× its budget worldwide in 1991–1995 and 3.0× in 2021–2025. That's before marketing and the theaters' cut.

    03

    A narrower slate

    Superhero and animated films went from 3% of these films' worldwide gross in the 1980s to 42% in 2021–2025. Comedy fell from 26% to 1.5%.

    04

    Sequels own the top 10

    Sequels and follow-ups made up 20% of each year's 10 biggest films in 1991–1995 and 76% in 2021–2025.

    Critics' scores are in the cost view for reference. Most reviews of 1980s and early-1990s films were written years later, so I don't compare eras on them.

    Beyond the movies

    Why this matters for organizations.

    A studio slate is a portfolio, and these charts are portfolio questions.

    Mix and concentration. When two genres carry over 40% of revenue, a slow year for either one shows up on the bottom line. Product lines, customer segments, and regions work the same way: check what share of the total a few names carry before something changes.

    Cost creep against return. Budgets went up and the gross-to-budget multiple came down. Track unit cost next to the return it earns, not on its own.

    Key-person dependence. The lead-actor view is a concentration chart too, the same check you'd run on a top account manager, supplier, or customer.

    Data and method

    Where the numbers come from.

    Which films
    Every film on Wikipedia's "List of year box office number-one films in the United States" pages, 1980–2025: weekend #1 films plus each page's top-grossing table (sourced to Box Office Mojo). That's 1,440 films, 17–46 a year. Re-releases of older films are dropped.
    Box office
    Lifetime worldwide gross from each film's Wikipedia infobox (1,438 films), including re-releases, as of 2026-10. Films still in theaters can rise.
    Budget
    Production budget from the infobox, in U.S. dollars only (1,416 films). Ranges like "$150–200 million" use the midpoint (295 films have a range for budget or gross). When an infobox lists both gross and net (after-incentive) budgets, I use net. Marketing isn't included.
    Genre
    One primary genre per film, taken from Wikipedia's first-sentence description (for example, "a 1990 American Christmas comedy film"). Animated and superhero films get those genres first. Otherwise the first match wins, in this order: sci-fi & fantasy, horror, action & adventure, thriller & crime, comedy, drama. 1,429 films are classified that way. The other 11 fall back to Wikidata genre tags. Real films blend genres, so treat the lines between genres as soft.
    Sequels
    A film counts as a sequel or follow-up if Wikidata says it "follows" an earlier film, or its Wikipedia intro calls it a sequel, prequel, spin-off, or numbered installment (489 films). Both sources miss some, and remakes and reboots aren't counted, so treat the share as a floor.
    Lead actor
    The first person in the infobox "Starring" list (top-billed). For animated films, that's the first voice credit.
    Critics
    Rotten Tomatoes score from Wikidata (1,426 films).
    Inflation
    BLS CPI-U, U.S. city average, all items (CUUR0000SA0), annual averages, rebased to 2025. The 2025 average uses 11 months because October 2025 wasn't collected. Adjusting worldwide grosses by U.S. inflation is an approximation.
    Limitations
    These are the hits, not every release. Films that never reached #1 or the top 10 aren't here, so this says nothing about the average movie. It's also survivorship-biased toward successes, which flatters the return numbers. Ratings for 1980s and early-1990s films are mostly retrospective reviews counted years later. 2020 is a pandemic year.

    Data: Wikipedia (CC BY-SA), Wikidata (CC0), BLS CPI. Built by me with AI, with a reproducible Python build script. Charts are vanilla JS and SVG, with no chart library.
