The story · Home-improvement retail

From hours to what sells.

This came after the staffing work. Once stores were staffed right, the next question was what was being sold, and by whom. I developed the Sales Performance tool, a proprietary application that monitored employee performance and set sales goals on its own.

More than the top line. Margin, attachment rate, service attachment, credit attachment, lead generation, and basket mix all mattered, not just total sales.

Sales through the specialists. Our specialists were the most trained people in the building, so we wanted the bulk of sales to route through them.

Goals from the forecast. Every goal came from the store and departmental forecasts, so it reflected real opportunity.

Twelve months of history. Employees and their managers could review performance together and talk about where to improve. Managers always had a list of who had the most room to grow. Not in a bad way, just a clear view of where the opportunity was.

The demo below is not that tool and uses no company data. It's one example store with fictional specialists, built to show how the pieces fit.

    Example assumptions

    How it works: department forecast = store forecast × department mix. Each specialist's goal = department forecast × the share of department sales we want through specialists, split by scheduled hours. The 12-month histories are generated from fictional profiles and a seasonal curve; they are not real people or real results. The opportunity list ranks each specialist's largest shortfall over the last three months.

    Beyond the store

    Why this matters for organizations.

    Goals, history, and coaching that all come from the same forecast tell one consistent story to everyone on the floor.

    01

    Quality of sales

    Margin, attachments, and basket mix show whether a sale helped the business, not just whether it happened.

    02

    Goals people trust

    When a goal traces back to the store and department forecast, it feels earned, not arbitrary.

    03

    Coaching, not blame

    An opportunity list starts the conversation with a strength and one focus area. It points managers where help pays off most.

    04

    History makes it fair

    Twelve months of trend separates a slow month from a real pattern, for the employee and the manager.

    05

    Right work, right person

    Routing big-ticket sales through the most trained people puts expertise where it changes the outcome for the customer.

    06

    Beyond retail

    Sales teams, branches, contact centers: any team with a forecast can cascade goals, track quality, and coach to the gaps.
