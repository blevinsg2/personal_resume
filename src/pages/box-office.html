    From the data

    More money, fewer kinds of movies.

    My takeaway: we pay far more for a narrower slate of movies that earn less per dollar. Every number here is calculated from the films on this page, in {{bo_base}} dollars. Nothing is estimated.

    Beyond the movies

    Why this matters for organizations.

    A studio slate is a portfolio, and these charts are portfolio questions.

    Mix and concentration. When two genres carry over 40% of revenue, a slow year for either one shows up on the bottom line. Product lines, customer segments, and regions work the same way: check what share of the total a few names carry before something changes.

    Cost creep against return. Budgets went up and the gross-to-budget multiple came down. Track unit cost next to the return it earns, not on its own.

    Key-person dependence. The lead-actor view is a concentration chart too, the same check you'd run on a top account manager, supplier, or customer.

    Data and method

    Where the numbers come from.

    Which films
    Every film on Wikipedia's "List of year box office number-one films in the United States" pages, {{bo_y0}}–{{bo_y1}}: weekend #1 films plus each page's top-grossing table (sourced to Box Office Mojo). That's {{bo_n}} films, {{bo_per_min}}–{{bo_per_max}} a year. Re-releases of older films are dropped.
    Box office
    Lifetime worldwide gross from each film's Wikipedia infobox ({{bo_n_ww}} films), including re-releases, as of {{bo_gen}}. Films still in theaters can rise.
    Budget
    Production budget from the infobox, in U.S. dollars only ({{bo_n_bud}} films). Ranges like "$150–200 million" use the midpoint ({{bo_n_rng}} films have a range for budget or gross). When an infobox lists both gross and net (after-incentive) budgets, I use net. Marketing isn't included.
    Genre
    One primary genre per film, taken from Wikipedia's first-sentence description (for example, "a 1990 American Christmas comedy film"). Animated and superhero films get those genres first. Otherwise the first match wins, in this order: sci-fi & fantasy, horror, action & adventure, thriller & crime, comedy, drama. {{bo_n_gs}} films are classified that way. The other {{bo_n_gf}} fall back to Wikidata genre tags. Real films blend genres, so treat the lines between genres as soft.
    Sequels
    A film counts as a sequel or follow-up if Wikidata says it "follows" an earlier film, or its Wikipedia intro calls it a sequel, prequel, spin-off, or numbered installment ({{bo_n_sq}} films). Both sources miss some, and remakes and reboots aren't counted, so treat the share as a floor.
    Lead actor
    The first person in the infobox "Starring" list (top-billed). For animated films, that's the first voice credit.
    Critics
    Rotten Tomatoes score from Wikidata ({{bo_n_rt}} films).
    Inflation
    BLS CPI-U, U.S. city average, all items (CUUR0000SA0), annual averages, rebased to {{bo_base}}. The 2025 average uses 11 months because October 2025 wasn't collected. Adjusting worldwide grosses by U.S. inflation is an approximation.
    Limitations
    These are the hits, not every release. Films that never reached #1 or the top 10 aren't here, so this says nothing about the average movie. It's also survivorship-biased toward successes, which flatters the return numbers. Ratings for 1980s and early-1990s films are mostly retrospective reviews counted years later. 2020 is a pandemic year.
